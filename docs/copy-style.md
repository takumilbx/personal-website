# Copy style: what well-loved personal sites do

Researched 2026-09-26 through web search (direct page fetches were blocked in the build environment, so this works from search summaries and published taglines, not full pages). Use this for every piece of copy on the site.

## Sites studied

| Person | How they describe themselves | What to borrow |
|---|---|---|
| Craig Mod (craigmod.com) | "Writer + Photographer"; "a writer, photographer, and walker living in Tokyo" | Two or three roles, the last one a human twist ("walker"). Physical, oddly specific details: pizza toast, 1,000 km along the Nakasendo, kissaten. |
| Maggie Appleton (maggieappleton.com) | Designer, anthropologist, and developer; she "sits at the intersection of design, anthropology, and web development, which are at the core of everything she makes" | Many roles joined by one stated thread. Currently and previously, in one line each. |
| Josh W. Comeau (joshwcomeau.com) | "Whimsical developer, educator"; "software developer, educator, and amateur pianist" | A playful adjective and an unexpected last item. Teaching as part of the identity. |
| Amelia Wattenberger (wattenberger.com) | "Developer, designer, and data translator"; loves "creating things in any medium, be it laser cutting, cooking, or coding" | Invented role words that explain what she really does ("data translator"). Hobbies listed next to the work. |
| Derek Sivers (sive.rs/about) | A long first-person story: decided at 14 to become a musician, lived 7 months in a town of 2, loves working 6am to 10pm | Story before claim. Numbers that are strange enough to remember. |

## The rules that follow

1. **Many roles, one thread.** List the roles plainly, then say what connects them. Never pick one role and hide the rest.
2. **End the list with a twist.** The last role or detail should be human or unexpected.
3. **One strange, concrete detail per block.** A number, a place, an object. "Some within hours of the Japanese broadcast" beats "fast turnaround".
4. **First person, plain words, short sentences.** Present tense for now, past tense for done.
5. **Currently, previously.** One line each, no job-description lists.
6. **Headings that say something.** Not "About me", "Services", "Projects" if a line with a point of view fits.
7. **Leave a question open.** Each section should make the reader want the next one.
8. **From the voice charter:** real numbers, the Thailand and Japan throughline, no "passionate about", no em-dashes.

## Applied so far

- About paragraph: roles listed, then the thread ("It looks like four jobs. It's really one").
- What I do: renamed "Four jobs, one thread"; four verb-led items (Translate, Research, Create, Build), each with one concrete detail. Film and photography folded into Create so the count matches the About paragraph.
- Still to apply: hero footer lines (on hold with the hero), case-study summaries, the About page, the Creator page.
