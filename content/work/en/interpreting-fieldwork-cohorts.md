## Context and stakeholders

By July 2022 I had been translating Japanese-to-Thai subtitles for bilibili for a year and a half, on the way to 70+ series, and had been a volunteer Japanese teacher at Wat Ang Keaw School with the Saturday School Foundation since December 2018. Both were about carrying Japanese into Thai for people who would never meet the source. Field interpreting is the opposite: a Japanese student and a Thai host in the same room, and me in between. That month I started freelance work as a Japanese, Thai and English translator and interpreter.

On one side are the visiting Japanese high school and university groups, roughly 20 students a cohort, and the institutions that send them. On the other are the Thai sites and the people who host the visits and answer the questions. The other half of the practice is simultaneous trilingual interpreting for speakers at international summits, on education, technology, sustainability and renewable energy, entertainment and Thai ethnic studies.

## The problem

A fieldwork visit fails quietly. The student asks in Japanese, the host answers in Thai, and what reaches the notebook is whatever survived the gap. Interpreting the words is the smaller half. The larger half is research support: making sure the question asked is the one the student meant, and that what comes back is usable as evidence when they get home. Every year a new cohort of roughly 20 starts from zero.

## What I did

For four successive annual cohorts of roughly 20 Japanese high school and university students each, I have carried the group through its site visits and fieldwork in Thailand, interpreting across Japanese, Thai and English and supporting the research behind each visit. Around that I do written translation of education, technical and cultural material.

## What the evidence showed

The evidence the record gives is repeat engagement: the groups have come back four years running, and each year I carry the next roughly 20 students through, about 80 students in all. It is also part of where I learned the observation and interviewing I now use in my own fieldwork in Japanese schools for my master's thesis, through the discipline of not letting the interpreter's guess become the data.
