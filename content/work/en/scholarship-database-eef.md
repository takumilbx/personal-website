## Context and stakeholders

The second project of my 2023 internship at the Office of the Deputy Governor, Bangkok Metropolitan Administration, was built for someone outside city hall. The Equitable Education Fund needed a national scholarship database, and the Education Policy Team, where I sat as a Data Visualization Intern, built it for them. That same summer I was sorting the city's 47 education policies into 8 focus areas, a view of the system from above. This question sat much closer to the students.

Four groups had something at stake. The Equitable Education Fund itself, which needed to see the whole field of funding in one place. The 564 funding organisations whose scholarships would be listed side by side. The Office of the Deputy Governor, which had taken the work on. And the students the scholarships were supposed to reach, low-income students above all, who are the reason an equity fund exists.

## The problem

There was no single national database that listed Thailand's scholarships together with who could apply for each one. Without it, the Equitable Education Fund could not see the whole field of funding, and it could not check whether the rules attached to that funding matched the students it was trying to reach. A scholarship a student cannot qualify for is not, for that student, a scholarship at all.

## What I did

I built the database covering 564 funding organisations and more than 700 scholarships, and structured it so that eligibility conditions could be read across scholarships rather than one at a time. Then I used it to look for eligibility gaps: patterns in the rules that would leave particular groups of students out, whatever the intention behind the rule.

## What the evidence showed

The clearest gap was a minimum-GPA requirement. Written as a filter for merit, it worked in practice as a filter for circumstance, and it shut out many low-income students, the very group an equity fund is there for. A student who works after school, or has no quiet place to study, carries a lower GPA for reasons that say nothing about whether they would use the money well.
