## Context and stakeholders

In the summer of 2025, mentoring the BKK Ranger Youth Council on youth-led policy proposals from the Deputy Governor's office at the Bangkok Metropolitan Administration, I saw how far a good idea sits from a policy someone can act on. That distance is what Seaizen: Urban Youth for the Blue Economy asked me to teach. From December 2025, in my first year at the University of Tokyo, I was Curriculum Designer for the project, run by the Thailand–United States Alumni Association under YSEALI YOUnified Beyond.

The stakeholders were the 50 students at the forum, urban youth asked to think about the blue economy; the alumni association as host and YSEALI YOUnified Beyond behind it; UNDP and the Thailand Policy Lab, whose tools I borrowed; and the follow-on workshop's participants, each arguing from a stakeholder role.

## The problem

Fifty students, 75 minutes, and a topic, the blue economy, that is easy to care about and hard to turn into policy. Explaining the problem would leave them with facts; asking for solutions with no method would leave them with slogans. I needed a method they could hold in 75 minutes, Thai cases they would recognise, and a way to know afterwards whether anyone had learned anything.

## What I did

I built the 75-minute session on three frames: UNDP's Data to Policy Navigator for the route from evidence to a decision, the Thailand Policy Lab Handbook for how policy is made here, and Kingdon's three streams for why some problems reach the agenda and others wait. I grounded each in Thai cases, including Traffy Fondue and TPMAP, so the method would not feel imported. Then I designed the follow-on stakeholder-role workshop and measured learning with pre- and post-tests.

## What the evidence showed

The stakeholder-role workshop has a measured result: by the end of it, over 80% of participants could explain the link between ocean acidification and GDP loss on the post-test. It is a number I can stand behind, rarer in learning design than it should be. The project ran from December 2025 to April 2026; what the 50 students produced in the forum is not written up here yet.
