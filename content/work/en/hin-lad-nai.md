## Context and stakeholders

Hin Lad Nai is a Karen ethnic village in Chiang Rai, recognised as a model of environmental stewardship. In September 2024, after flooding and landslides there, the Thailand–United States Alumni Association ran a youth camp in the village, with me as Activity & Content Lead. In November the work moved to Bangkok: From Village to Vision, a workshop at theCOMMONS Thonglor on the village's sustainable-living practices, where I led PR and content. One village, two audiences, five months.

The camp's stakeholders were the local students whose learning needs we designed around, the villagers and community stakeholders we consulted, the volunteers, and the watershed forest itself, our learning space. The workshop added 80+ applicants, the 30+ who got a place, and the association, whose brand guidelines and Instagram I had helped set up from April 2023.

## The problem

A flood damages a place and its people, and students need somewhere to put what happened. The camp had to support psychosocial recovery and still be a camp, built around what local students needed rather than what visitors assumed. In Bangkok the problem turned around: how do you get people in Thonglor to apply for a workshop about a village in Chiang Rai?

## What I did

For the camp I designed a three-day programme in consultation with community stakeholders, each activity built around the local students' learning needs, with the watershed forest as the learning space. Every day I ran a reflection and evaluation session with students, villagers and volunteers, for psychosocial recovery and for the next cohort's programme. I had interpreted for Japanese student groups on Thai fieldwork by then, roughly 20 a cohort, and knew what a visit looks like when visitors set the agenda. I did not want that here.

For the workshop I led PR and content design, the key visual and the social campaign, produced a short documentary about the village as the learning material, and facilitated the icebreaking sessions on the day.

## What the evidence showed

The number I can stand behind is the workshop's: the key visual and social campaign drew more than 80 applicants for 30+ places. For the camp, the evidence lived in the daily reflection and evaluation sessions. I have not put figures on it here.
