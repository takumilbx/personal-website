## Context and stakeholders

I came to this project through the Impact Leaders Program of the School of Leadership and Khon Thai Foundation: two months of systems thinking, design thinking and theory of change, then a four-month practicum with the Bangkok Metropolitan Administration. That practicum was my internship, May to August 2025, as a Policy & Communications Intern on the Secretariat team in the Office of the Deputy Governor. A proposed update to the Bangkok Metropolitan Administration Act (1985) had gone out for public feedback, and 10,849 submissions had come back.

The stakeholders ran from the street to the council chamber: the residents who wrote those submissions; the Deputy Governor and his policy team, with an agenda of their own; the policymakers who would act on an executive summary; the public who would read the press releases; and the city council, for whom I also helped build a public platform for residents' policy proposals. The same four months took me on 50+ field visits with the Deputy Governor, to schools, child development centres, parks and sports fields, and on my own into schools for teacher and student feedback on the city's Digital Classroom programme, the Bangkok side of the comparison with Japan's GIGA School Program I had carried since my senior thesis. A city speaks in more than one register.

## The problem

Ten thousand submissions is more than a small team can read closely by hand, and skimming them defeats the purpose of asking. The office needed to know where residents' priorities diverged from the agenda already on the table, in a form policymakers could use and the public could read.

## What I did

I analysed all 10,849 submissions using NLP and large language models, and surfaced where residents' priorities diverged from the policy agenda. Then I wrote the results up twice: as an executive summary for the office, and as press releases that policymakers could use and the public could follow without a briefing.

## What the evidence showed

The analysis showed where residents' priorities diverged from the policy agenda, and those points of divergence went into the executive summary and the press releases. What the record does not yet hold is which themes diverged, and by how much.
