## Context and stakeholders

I joined Edsy, an EdTech startup building an AI-assisted English tutor, in December 2024 as Business Development Executive for Japan and International Markets. The year before, my senior thesis had put me through Japan's GIGA School Program on paper and a Bangkok Metropolitan Administration classroom in person, so I arrived already comparing the two systems. Selling into one of them was new.

The stakeholders ran well beyond Edsy's UX/UI and engineering teams and the local teams I recruited: the pilot-list schools, the boards of education and corporate clients we pitched, partner companies and organisations in each market, Gakken and its Turkey operation, and the ministries of education whose textbooks define what an English lesson should look like. Underneath them all were the learners.

## The problem

An AI English tutor does not travel on its own. Each of the four countries teaches English to its own national curriculum, has its own regulators and competitors, and the school, board of education or company being pitched wants to see its own textbook in the demo, not a generic lesson. The application itself also had to exist in Japanese, Korean and Turkish.

## What I did

Japan came first. I validated the entry through regulatory and competitive analysis, built a targeted pilot-school list and recruited local teams, then supported partnership discussions with Gakken, one of Japan's largest education companies, and its Turkey operation, working in Japanese. Then I took the same model into Korea, Turkey and Saudi Arabia, establishing strategic partnerships with partner companies and organisations across all four. For every market I built AI-generated demo English lessons localised against the national curriculum using that ministry's textbooks, then tailored each demo to the specific school, board or company being pitched. I also worked with the UX/UI and engineering teams on localising the application into Japanese, Korean and Turkish, bringing market and language context into their decisions.

## What the evidence showed

Within nine months the model went from one validated market to four, with partnerships established in each, partnership discussions under way with Gakken, demo lessons matched to four national curricula and the application in three new languages. What I cannot yet put a number on is what happened in the classrooms once the demo was over.
