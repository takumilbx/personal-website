## Context and stakeholders

I had volunteered with Saturday School Foundation since December 2018, teaching Japanese at Wat Ang Keaw School in Bangkok, so I knew its classrooms from the front of the room before I joined as a Project Development Intern. In June 2022 the foundation, an educational non-profit, began a three-month pilot with Sea (Garena) Thailand: an e-sports classroom across 8 Bangkok schools. It was my job to manage it.

At stake were 245 students and 25 teachers in those 8 schools, Sea (Garena) Thailand as the delivery partner, the industry speakers we brought in, and the foundation itself, which would hold the final report. E-sports in a school is never only a student question. Teachers have to be willing to host it, a game company has to deliver, and someone has to write down what actually happened.

## The problem

E-sports reaches students as a game, not as an industry. There are careers behind every match, but a school timetable has no unit that names them, and a lecture about jobs would not hold 245 students for three months. The pilot had to teach the industry in a form the students would actually run, and it had to work in 8 schools with 25 teachers, not in one classroom with one enthusiast.

## What I did

I planned the curriculum around five careers in the e-sports industry, and rather than five lectures I designed the whole programme to be delivered through a student-run tournament, so that students met each career through the work the tournament needed. I recruited the industry speakers, coordinated with Sea (Garena) Thailand as the delivery partner, and managed the pilot across the 8 schools from June to August 2022. Every session went into the pilot's final report, which I documented as we went.

## What the evidence showed

By the end of the three months the pilot had reached 245 students and 25 teachers across 8 Bangkok schools, and every session was on record in the final report. That is reach, not a learning result. The report is the artefact that would show what students took from the tournament, and I have not put its findings here yet.
