## Context and stakeholders

For four months in 2025 I was the Deputy Governor's field record. I went with him on 50+ visits to schools, child development centres, parks and sports fields across Bangkok, and photographed and wrote up each one for his public channels. After enough school visits you stop seeing classrooms and start seeing ratios: one adult at the front, and a number of children in front of them that changes from school to school.
<!-- TODO owner: confirm this is how it felt, or replace it with the honest turning point. -->

The people with a stake in that number sit at different distances from it. Closest are the teachers in the schools where staffing runs thinnest, and the students in front of them. Then the schools themselves, the BMA public schools whose data the work was built from. Furthest away, and with the most power to change the number, is the Deputy Governor's office and the executives who make decisions on teacher-student ratios across the city, which is who the dashboard was built for. I was a Policy & Communications Intern on the Secretariat team in the Office of the Deputy Governor, working with the Education, Social Development and Cultural Policy Team.

## The problem

A single teacher-student ratio for the whole city hides the schools where the problem actually lives. The office needed to make decisions on teacher-student ratios across the city, and for that it needed the ratio school by school, on a map, in a form an executive could read directly. That view did not yet exist.

## What I did

Working from BMA public-school data, I calculated teacher-student ratios across the city's public schools. I mapped the results in ArcGIS, so the pattern could be read as geography rather than as a table, and built a Power BI dashboard on top of it that showed where staffing ran thinnest. This was my part of a larger executive-facing school dashboard the office was building to support those staffing decisions.

## What the evidence showed

The dashboard showed where staffing ran thinnest across BMA public schools, and it went to the Deputy Governor's office as support for decisions on teacher-student ratios across the city. A ratio is a number about one school, but staffing is a decision about a city, and the map is what lets the second follow from the first.
